## 1. Consent to the operation of the service (required)

By ticking the boxes when entering the app, I declare that:

- I am at least **18 years old**;
- I have read the Terms of Use and the FLIRT Privacy and Personal Data Processing Policy;
- I agree to my data being processed for operating my account, displaying my profile, recommending suitable profiles, conversations, events and the loyalty program, under the conditions described in the Policy.

Some of this processing has a legal basis other than consent (contract, legal obligation or legitimate interest), as shown in the table in section 5 of the Policy.

## 2. Explicit consent to the processing of sensitive data (required for recommendations)

My gender, the genders I am interested in and my dating status may reveal my sexual orientation or aspects of my intimate life. These are special categories of data (Art. 9).

**I give my explicit consent** for this data to be processed **only** to recommend suitable profiles to me and to show my profile to people I may be a suitable match for, under the following conditions:

- the genders I am interested in are not shown to other users;
- the data is not used for advertising and is not sold;
- I can change it in my profile at any time.

Without this data FLIRT cannot recommend people to me. If I do not agree, I cannot use the recommendations feature. I can withdraw my consent by deleting my account.

## 3. AI features (optional, off by default)

If I turn on AI features in Settings, I agree that:

- for **message suggestions**, at most the last 8 messages of the open conversation are sent to the AI provider, each truncated to 400 characters, without names, identifiers or photos and with contact details masked;
- for the **"chemistry" explanation**, the compatibility score, shared interests, languages and dating statuses and the approximate distance are sent;
- the processing is carried out by OpenRouter, Inc. and by the model provider (Anthropic PBC), in the United States of America, as processors of the controller.

If I turn off the toggle, nothing more is sent to the AI provider. Refusing has no other consequence.

## 4. Notifications via the Telegram bot (optional)

The bot @flrt_party_bot can message me only after I allow it to in Telegram. If I allow it, I agree to receive notifications about new messages, likes, matches and new stories. Notifications may contain the other person's first name and a preview of at most 80 characters of the message (with contact details masked) and are transmitted through Telegram's servers.

I can turn off each type of notification at any time in Settings → notifications or block the bot in Telegram.

## 5. Face verification (optional; biometric data)

*The feature is currently unavailable. Consent is requested separately, only if you choose to use it.*

If I tick the separate box for face verification, I give my **explicit consent** for:

- the selfie I take in the app to be automatically compared with my profile photo, to confirm that the profile belongs to me and for me to receive the "verified" badge;
- the comparison to be carried out by the facial recognition service provider (Amazon Web Services Rekognition, when the service is enabled), as a processor of the controller.

**The selfie is not stored.** Only the verification result is kept. Refusing has no consequence, except that the profile does not receive the "verified" badge.

## 6. Withdrawal of consent and my rights

- I can withdraw the optional consents (3, 4 and 5) at any time, as easily as I gave them: in Settings, in "Privacy & data" or by e-mail.
- Consents 1 and 2 are necessary for the operation of the service. I can withdraw them by deleting my account (Settings → "Delete account") or by writing to the controller.
- Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before the withdrawal.
- The controller keeps proof of my consent: the document, version, date and time, IP address and user agent.
- I have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, as well as the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing (Art. 15–22).
